Still, at least I wasn’t in the cooler with Sam and Tommy. I stared up, counting the pieces of wood along the bedframe. Somebody coughed nearby, and somebody else started to snore, which Huntley must have found distasteful as the silence was soon broken by his sharp voice cursing the man. I couldn’t stay confined to the barracks tonight; staying in any one place for too long drove me slightly insane, let alone here. About the Author

Haunted by the memories of his turbulent childhood in Hong Kong, Stephen is forced to confront the shadows that have followed him for years. What began as innocent moments in a bustling family home soon gave way to unexplainable events: echoes in empty rooms, strange whispers in the night, and a power within him that he could neither understand nor control. As time goes by, the line between memory and nightmare blurs, and Stephen must face the ghosts of his past before they consume him entirely. Told with chilling intimacy and emotional depth, this debut novel by British author Stephen Lee, originally from Hong Kong, delves into the haunting intersection of trauma, identity, and the supernatural. Lee crafts a story that lingers long after the final page, inviting readers into a world where fear is both deeply personal and profoundly universal, a psychological horror that will ingrain in your mind for years to come.

Books Description

The impact of inter-generational family trauma can never be underestimated, and it is this lived experience which drove British author Stephen Lee to explore the supernatural in his first novel. Set against the turbulent background of a Hong Kong childhood filled with nightmares, shadows and haunting visions, a “parallel life” narrative paints a vivid portrait of Hong Kong in wartime - and the ghosts of the deadly conflict which rest unquietly… just below the surface of its contemporary streets.
