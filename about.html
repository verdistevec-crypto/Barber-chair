Press Release

United Kingdom – Moving between modern times, the Second World War, and the 1970s, “A Barber Chair” explores family dynamics and how these are influenced by post-traumatic experiences, through the medium of a compulsive and haunting supernatural thriller that will live on in the reader’s memory. “Behind a dream lies a shadow,” believes the novel’s British author, Stephen Lee. In narrative order. Stephen looks back on the tragic events of his childhood in Hong Kong, which compelled him to write this book. Through flashbacks, he first remembers a nightmare he had about his Aunt Charlotte combing the hair of a corpse in the basement of his home that looked like a mortuary, followed by a haunting that causes a servant to quit when she sees a face stained on a wall. His cousin Joe argues with his father, after which Stephen has an astral travelling dream. The family is then visited by a strange man, who offers to buy the house. The house is broken into, but the burglars do not manage to steal much. The day after the burglary, a banquet is held at the house where several strange events happen. What follows is a series of hauntings, including the disappearance of a two-million-dollar diamond ring, the death of the family pets, a car spitting out black tar from the exhaust, and other events that lead the family to seek help. The help that then comes is a mystic figure named Madame Neuma, who expels the spirit that haunts the house. The book then shifts focus to Joe’s life in a boarding school in England, where he hears of a suspicious death of his best-friend and roommate RAF Cadet Gerald. Shortly after, Joe experiences a tragedy long foretold by one of Stephen’s dreams. The book then culminates in an exploration of family grief – a narrative that is told as a parallel plot alongside that of Cadet William – a British RAF cadet burdened with the horrors of war. He has a tragic love affair in Hong Kong at the height of the Japanese invasion, which results in the mysterious death of his lover, Freya. After being captured and subsequently escaping from the Japanese, Cadet William finds Freya’s dead body. He promptly returns to a barber shop that his parents left for him in Hong Kong, and whilst reflecting upon his experience, a stray bomb from a B-25 US bomber destroys the barber shop, whose location uncovers the many unexplained events that happened throughout Stephen’s young family life.

About the Author

Haunted by the memories of his turbulent childhood in Hong Kong, Stephen is forced to confront the shadows that have followed him for years. What began as innocent moments in a bustling family home soon gave way to unexplainable events: echoes in empty rooms, strange whispers in the night, and a power within him that he could neither understand nor control. As time goes by, the line between memory and nightmare blurs, and Stephen must face the ghosts of his past before they consume him entirely.
